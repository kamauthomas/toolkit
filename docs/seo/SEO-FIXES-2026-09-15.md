## Homepage performance follow-up — 16 September 2026

A throttled mobile browser trace against production reproduced the slow hero more precisely than the external snapshot. It recorded a `441 ms` TTFB, `2.95 s` DOMContentLoaded, `6.56 s` load event, `0.0086` CLS and an `8.40 s` LCP. The low observed CLS means the earlier `0.4735` result is not consistently reproducible; the LCP regression was reproducible and had an identifiable cause.

The carousel changed from slide one to slide two before the slow mobile trace settled. That made slide two the final LCP element and caused all three mobile hero backgrounds to transfer. The trace also found a `260,811` byte decorative `iskill-banner-04.jpg`, three legacy icon stylesheets, and late Sina carousel/tooltip scripts even though the rebuilt homepage renders none of the corresponding legacy widgets.

Release `2026.09.16.1` therefore:

- renders the hero as responsive `<picture>`/`<img>` media with explicit dimensions and high priority only on the first image;
- keeps later slide URLs in inert data attributes until the slide is requested;
- disables autoplay on mobile and for reduced-motion users, while retaining manual navigation;
- recompresses the three mobile hero images from `400,016` to `278,126` bytes in total;
- removes the low-value 260 KB Quick Links background; and
- dequeues unused legacy icon assets and late Sina scripts on rebuilt surfaces.

A sandboxed mobile fixture verified that slide one remains the LCP candidate with zero fixture CLS, later slides remain without `src` after the autoplay interval, the mobile pause control is absent, and manual navigation still hydrates and activates the requested slide. The release was then deployed demo-first and promoted to production. A throttled demo trace recorded `0.0016` CLS and an approximately `2.6 s` LCP, loaded only the 720 px first mobile hero, and found none of the retired Sina/Owl scripts. Desktop and mobile renders were visually accepted before production promotion.

## Generative and agentic search strategy

Google’s current guidance says its AI features use the normal Search index and ranking systems. A page must be crawlable, indexed and eligible for a snippet. Google explicitly says `llms.txt`, special AI markup, artificial “chunking,” and content rewritten only for AI do not improve its generative Search visibility. The useful strategy is therefore:

- server-render complete answers in canonical HTML;
- publish unique, first-hand workshop, trainer, learner and outcome evidence;
- use descriptive headings and internal links;
- keep facts, schema and visible content consistent;
- make relevant images and video crawlable;
- remove duplicate and low-value URLs;
- monitor the Search Console Generative AI report and query fan-out topics.

Bing likewise connects ordinary crawling, indexing, canonicalisation, content clarity and authority with Copilot/grounding eligibility. Add IndexNow through a managed webmaster integration after ownership and key handling are assigned; do not hard-code a private IndexNow key into the theme.

Primary references:

- [Google: Optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)
- [Bing IndexNow](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/indexnow-0z209wby)
